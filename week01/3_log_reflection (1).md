## What the data can show

The dataset contains recorded prices for four selected teaching items across two city labels and twelve dates. It can be used to compare the average recorded price of the same item between the two cities and to identify which item has the greatest or smallest variation in recorded prices.

## What the data cannot show

The dataset cannot establish the overall cost of living in either city because it includes only four selected items and does not include other household expenses such as housing, transport, education, or healthcare. It also cannot explain why prices changed because it does not contain information about taxes, supply conditions, transport costs, or demand.

The observations are labelled as illustrative teaching data, not actual PBS observations. Therefore, the findings should be described as patterns in this dataset rather than as evidence about actual market prices.

## Most important limitation

A higher average across all items does not automatically mean that one city is more expensive overall. Different items have different price levels, so a fair comparison should examine each item separately and use matching dates where possible.

## Conclusion

This exercise shows why economic claims need to be made precise before they can be tested. The two questions in this assignment can be investigated with the available `item`, `city`, `date`, and `price` variables. The results can describe differences and variation in the recorded observations, but they cannot explain the causes of those patterns or represent the full cost of living.
